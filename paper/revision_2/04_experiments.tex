\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\subsubsection{Dataset and Evaluation Metrics}
We evaluate our method on the Intentonomy dataset \cite{jia_intentonomy_2021}, a multi-label benchmark for visual intention recognition in social media scenarios, using the standard split of 12,739 training, 498 validation, and 1,216 test images. Its training set provides soft agreement annotations with values in $\{0, 0.33, 0.66, 1\}$ that reflect annotator consensus and serve as the uncertainty signal $\omega$.

We report Macro, Micro, and Samples F1 and, following recent Intentonomy works, their average $\textit{Avg.}$; unless stated otherwise all numbers are percentages on the Intentonomy test split. Following Jia et al. \cite{jia_intentonomy_2021}, we also break intent classes down by difficulty, measured by how much a standard ResNet-50 model exceeds random performance, and by content dependency into object-dependent ({\itshape Object}), context-dependent ({\itshape Context}), and {\itshape Others}, which depend on both foreground and background. We emphasize the \textit{Hard} score throughout, since it measures the ambiguity-prone categories where semantic overlap and annotator disagreement are most severe.

\subsubsection{Implementation Details}

Our default backbone is frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 \cite{radford_learning_2021}, and the visual student is a lightweight residual MLP on top of a fixed SLR-C prior. In SLR-C, priors come from lexical intent phrases, canonical intent descriptions, and Gemini 3.1 Pro-generated scenario descriptions \cite{team_gemini_2025} encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder, their normalized logits averaged before local reranking within Top-10 visual candidates. For UTD, rationale text is generated offline by Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct \cite{bai_qwen25-vl_2025} and encoded by a frozen bge-large-en-v1.5 text encoder \cite{xiao_c-pack_2024}, with both prompt templates released with our artifacts. The teacher consumes only text embeddings and uses Asymmetric Loss \cite{ridnik_asymmetric_2021} ($\gamma_{-}=2$, $\gamma_{+}=0$, clipping 0.05); the student uses AdamW \cite{loshchilov_decoupled_2018} with learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 256, dropout 0.1, hidden dimensions 1,024 (teacher) and 768 (student), and distillation temperature 2.0. Best-epoch selection and early stopping use validation class-wise Macro F1, and all thresholds are fitted on the validation split and fixed before test evaluation, so test outputs never enter model or threshold selection. UTD introduces \emph{zero extra inference cost}, since the text teacher is discarded at test time.

\label{sec:sota_comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} summarizes the main comparison on the test split. \revised{Under the matched protocol FDIL leads the reproduced methods by $+3.3$ to $+5.8$ Avg.\ F1, and paired two-sided $t$-tests over the five shared seeds are significant after Holm correction on all four F1 metrics against every one of them; against the strongest, LabCR, the margin is $+3.32$ Avg.\ F1 ($p_{\mathrm{H}}=0.002$).} UTD alone already improves Avg.\ F1 at zero extra inference cost, and full FDIL is best on every column.

\subsection{Efficiency Comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_comparison} compares inference efficiency. FDIL (UTD only) costs exactly the baseline, since the text teacher is discarded after training, and full FDIL needs 2.41M parameters, 4.93M FLOPs, and 0.466\,ms per image, whereas HLEG introduces much heavier Transformer decoding. IntCLIP uses the fewest parameters of any head measured, though at higher FLOPs than FDIL, and its region-aggregation design runs on the same frozen encoder while trailing FDIL by $5.80$ Avg.\ F1 under the matched protocol of Table~\ref{tab:main_results}. HVU-CLIP releases no code and cannot be measured, while IntentMLM and CoT4Intent require MLLM inference and are therefore not included in the measured feature-level comparison. FDIL therefore achieves its gains with minor inference overhead on top of the shared backbone.
