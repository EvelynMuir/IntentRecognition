\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We study subjective visual intention recognition under supervisory and semantic ambiguity and propose FDIL, a functionally decoupled framework where fixed semantic priors provide structured candidate semantics, a residual student supports image-aware prediction on top of that prior, and uncertainty-aware text teachers stabilize ambiguous supervision. Experiments on Intentonomy show consistent improvements over strong visual baselines and prior state-of-the-art methods. \revised{Cross-dataset studies on the larger EMOTIC benchmark and on Flickr-LDL and Emotion6 show that the component-level division of labour is broadly preserved, with the semantic prior moving ranking and pointwise distribution fit and the uncertainty-guided teacher moving thresholded F1 and rank correlation, although the overall gains there are smaller.} Overall, these results suggest that, given strong visual representations, explicitly modelling supervisory and semantic ambiguity can further improve subjective intent recognition. We hope this decoupled formulation offers a clearer perspective for future research on human-centered visual understanding.
