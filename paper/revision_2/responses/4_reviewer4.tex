\cresp{There must be larger-scale datasets to better evaluate the proposed methods.}
{\gist{We agree, and the manuscript now evaluates the larger EMOTIC benchmark, together with two label-distribution benchmarks that broaden the evaluation along a second axis. EMOTIC provides a larger transfer benchmark, although the ambiguity-aware evaluation is necessarily restricted to its \res{$10{,}613$}-person multi-annotated pool.}

\rh{Scale.} Sec.~\ref{sec:emotic} adds EMOTIC~\cite{kosti_context_2019}, with \res{$23{,}571$} images and \res{$34{,}320$} annotated instances, which keeps the multi-label output geometry our method is defined on. Because multi-annotator labels are released only for validation and test, the ambiguity-aware evaluation is confined to that multi-annotated pool of \res{$10{,}613$} persons, on which we run a controlled $5\times5$ cross-validation. Each seed aggregates its complete five-fold out-of-fold performance over the pool, so a seed is one paired observation and every test uses five paired values. Full FDIL improves the baseline by \res{$+0.42$} Macro, \res{$+0.67$} Micro, \res{$+0.62$} Samples F1 and \res{$+0.38$} mAP, each significant across seeds at $p\le0.005$, with the same division of labour, since gated UTD carries the thresholded F1 metrics and SLR-C carries mAP. We report this as a controlled transfer analysis on a held-out multi-annotated pool, not as a comparison against published EMOTIC numbers trained on the single-annotator training partition. Only SLR-C's candidate size and prior weight were retuned for the new label space (\res{$K=26$}, \res{$\alpha=0.09$}). As an additional check reported here, nested cross-validation of this selection gives \res{$42.69$} mAP, essentially reproducing the reported SLR-C result.

\rh{Breadth.} Sec.~\ref{sec:flickrldl} then evaluates Flickr-LDL (\res{$11{,}150$} images, per-image annotator vote distributions over eight emotions) and Emotion6 (\res{$1{,}980$} images, seven categories including neutral). Neither is individually larger than Intentonomy, and we do not present them as answering the scale request. They answer evaluation breadth, because label distribution learning removes the class-wise threshold search entirely, leaving only the two functional roles to carry the transfer. The scale and the breadth axes are therefore covered by different datasets. In our implementation, each uses its untouched official test split, five seeds with paired $t$-tests, and out-of-fold teacher predictions so no training target derives from an evaluated sample. On Flickr-LDL, FDIL significantly improves rank correlation and the divisiveness error but significantly degrades Clark distance, and on Emotion6 the only reliable change is Clark distance, the metric it loses on Flickr-LDL. We do not aggregate the two into a single favourable summary. What replicates on both is the division of labour the decoupling predicts, with SLR-C moving the pointwise-fit metrics and gated UTD moving rank correlation.

For completeness, Table~\ref{tab:ldl_letter} of this letter gives the seed dispersion, the per-metric $p$-values omitted from the manuscript table for space, and four matched published label-distribution objectives, $\delta$-LDL~\cite{li_approximately_2025}, LDL-LRR~\cite{jia_label_2023}, LDL-DPA~\cite{jia_adaptive_2024}, and LDL-DVS~\cite{lu2026divisiveness}, all of which stay essentially identical to the baseline under these strong frozen CLIP features.

\cresp{The paper lacks theoretical analysis or deeper learning insights. It must add more value to the paper if more theoretical analysis were conducted, e.g., from information-theoretical or feature selection perspectives.}
{\gist{We agree, and rebuilt Sec.~\ref{sec:decoupling_principle} around exactly those two perspectives, with Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} testing what they predict.}

\rh{Information-theoretic side.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy_decomp} treats the intent label as a random human judgement rather than a deterministic ground truth, giving the exact three-term identity $H(Y_c\mid B)=H(Y_c\mid X)+I(Y_c;X\mid Z)+I(Y_c;Z\mid B)$, which separates irreducible subjective disagreement, the intent-relevant information the frozen representation discards, and the information the baseline readout leaves unused. The middle term is unrecoverable downstream by the data-processing inequality, and we now say explicitly that FDIL does not address it. SLR-C's contribution is stated as a conditional-information claim, $H(Y_c\mid B,S)=H(Y_c\mid B)-I(Y_c;S\mid B)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmi_prior}), and because the prior is computed from the same frozen feature, this gain satisfies $I(Y_c;S\mid B)\le I(Y_c;Z\mid B)$, so SLR-C addresses readout insufficiency only. The gain vanishes when the prior is redundant given the baseline scores; SLR-C's improvement over the baseline is consistent with the prior supplying additional label-relevant information, and the prior ablation (Table~\ref{tab:prior_ablation}), where no single source dominates, indicates that the textual sources contribute complementary content.

\rh{Feature-selection side.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin_identity} formalizes the Top-$K$ stage as local hypothesis screening followed by margin correction. Inside the screened set, reranking shifts a confusable pair's margin by exactly $\alpha(\tilde{s}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{c^+}-\tilde{s}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{c^-})$, and nothing outside it changes. In screening terms $K$ trades coverage against distractor noise, and both ends are measured. The baseline Top-10 set covers \res{$83.9\%$} of positive labels, and the sensitivity study (Table~\ref{tab:k_sensitivity_test}) shows an interior optimum at $K=10$.

\rh{UTD as reliability-weighted privileged information.} The rationale teacher is now stated for what it is, a channel available in training and discarded at test time~\cite{vapnik_new_2009,lopez-paz_unifying_2016}. That turns the gate into an estimation question with an answer, because the mean-squared-error-optimal weight $g^{\ast}=\sigma_v^2(\omega)/(\sigma_v^2(\omega)+\sigma_t^2+b_t^2)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opt_gate} is monotonically decreasing in agreement, so our $g=1-\omega$ is a parameter-free monotone approximation of it rather than an unstructured heuristic. We are careful about the endpoint: at $\omega=1$ the observed supervision is maximally reliable rather than provably noiseless, and the manuscript notes that $g^{\ast}=0$ there would additionally require $\sigma_v^2(1)=0$, which we do not assume.

\rh{The experiment these derivations predict.} Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} stratifies samples by the two ambiguities and measures each module per cell, cross-fitted over the train split because it is the only split carrying per-sample agreement. The semantic axis confirms the prediction cleanly. The supervisory axis is directionally consistent but weaker, and its low-ambiguity stratum holds only $1{,}859$ images, so we claim no monotone trend. The full model also does not exceed its stronger component in the doubly-ambiguous cell. We therefore use the experiment to support the predicted localization of the two effects, not to claim a gain from composing them.

}

\cresp{The overall framework is over-complicated and lacks clarity in design justification. Multiple modules are introduced, but their individual contributions and interactions are not clearly disentangled. The ablation studies, while present, are not sufficient to demonstrate that each component is necessary rather than redundant, making the method appear over-engineered.}
{\gist{FDIL has two functional modules, only one of which exists at inference, each one dictated by a distinct term of the decomposition; the $2\times2$ ambiguity-stratified analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} disentangles their individual contributions, and four further tests separate necessity from redundancy.}

\rh{On complexity.} FDIL has two modules, a fixed semantic prior with a residual head (SLR-C) and a training-only text teacher (UTD). UTD's effect is retained in the student's parameters, but the teacher is discarded after training and adds no inference-time computation, so the deployed model is a lightweight residual MLP over frozen features at \res{$2.41$}M parameters and \res{$0.47$}\,ms per image at the head level (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_comparison}).

\rh{On design justification.} The two modules are not accumulated components but the two quantities the entropy decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy_decomp} leaves actionable. The readout-insufficiency term $I(Y_c;Z\mid B)$ is a property of the classifier head given a frozen representation, and SLR-C is the module that acts on it, by supplying conditional information from a fixed semantic prior (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmi_prior}) and correcting margins inside a screened candidate set (Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin_identity}). Unreliable annotation is instead a property of the supervision signal, not of the head, and is handled by treating rationales as privileged information whose optimal weight decreases in annotator agreement (Eq.~\eqref{eq:opt_gate}). Because the two act on different objects, one at inference on scores and one at training on targets, the decomposition motivates treating them with distinct mechanisms rather than forcing them into a shared target. This design choice is further supported empirically by the unified controls in Table~\ref{tab:unified_decoupled}.

\rh{On disentangling individual contributions.} The $2\times2$ ambiguity-stratified analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} was added for this purpose. It stratifies samples into a $2\times2$ grid by the two ambiguities, semantic ambiguity by the negative top-2 margin of out-of-fold baseline logits and supervisory ambiguity by $1-\omega$, and measures each module separately per cell against a shared baseline with paired $t$-tests and Holm correction over the twelve comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:quadrants}, reproduced in the Reviewer~2 block above). The two modules localize to different cells. SLR-C gains only where semantic ambiguity is high (\res{$+4.08$} and \res{$+1.17$} F1, both significant) and nothing in the low-semantic cells, so its effect tracks the margin axis independently of annotator agreement. UTD is inert wherever agreement is high (\res{$-0.05$} and \res{$-0.07$} F1) and its only significant gain is the doubly-ambiguous cell (\res{$+0.84$}, corrected $p=0.025$). These patterns are consistent with distinct functional sensitivities of the two modules. We also state what the same analysis does not support: in the doubly-ambiguous cell the full model tracks its stronger component (\res{$+1.14$} against SLR-C's \res{$+1.17$}) rather than exceeding it, so we claim the predicted localization of the two effects and not additive composition, and we note that the low-supervisory-ambiguity stratum holds only $1{,}859$ images.

\rh{On necessity rather than redundancy.} Beyond the stratified analysis, the manuscript offers four independent tests. The unified control shows that merging both signals into one distillation target performs no better than either alone (Table~\ref{tab:unified_decoupled}). Shuffling rationales across images is worse than removing UTD entirely (\res{$-10.40$} against \res{$-4.47$} Avg.\ F1), so the gain depends on image-specific content rather than on adding external text (Table~\ref{tab:negative_controls}). Removing or flattening the agreement gate costs \res{$-1.97$} and \res{$-1.12$} Avg.\ F1 and degrades every other aggregate metric, the one exception being that the uniform gate gains \res{$+1.13$} on the hard subset (Table~\ref{tab:negative_controls}). And the two label-distribution transfers separate the modules by which metric each one moves, on both datasets, where they cannot share a fitted threshold. These distinct response patterns provide evidence against simple redundancy between the two components.

\rh{Where a component is not clearly necessary.} FDIL's advantage over its own UTD-only ablation does not reach significance on Samples F1 at $n=5$ ($p_{\mathrm H}=0.0755$ in Table~\ref{tab:significance_letter}), although it does on Macro, Micro, Avg., mAP, and hard-subset F1. The case for the decoupled design therefore rests on the overall evidence together with modularity, since each function stays separately measurable and UTD can be deployed alone at zero additional inference cost.

\rh{Presentation and notation.} The unified control and the stratified analysis are now one subsection arguing effectiveness and necessity together, and every results subsection has been reduced to its table or figure, a short analysis, and a conclusion. The notation table is retained in Sec.~\ref{sec:method} (Table~\ref{tab:notation}) and reorganized into a compact two-column layout. Method-level notation is summarized there, while the additional symbols used only in the theoretical analysis are defined in words at their first occurrence.
