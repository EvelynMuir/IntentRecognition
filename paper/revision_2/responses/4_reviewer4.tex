\cresp{There must be larger-scale datasets to better evaluate the proposed methods.}
{\gist{We agree, and the cross-dataset evidence has moved from the letter into the manuscript as a full subsection.}

Sec.~\ref{sec:flickrldl} evaluates FDIL on Flickr-LDL (\res{$11{,}150$} images, per-image annotator vote distributions over eight emotions) and Emotion6 (\res{$1{,}980$} images, seven categories including neutral). Each uses its untouched official test split, five seeds with paired $t$-tests, and out-of-fold teacher predictions so no training target derives from an evaluated sample. We chose these to vary a different axis rather than simply to add images, because label distribution learning removes the class-wise threshold search entirely, leaving only the two functional roles to carry the transfer.

\rh{Results.} On Flickr-LDL, FDIL significantly improves rank correlation and the divisiveness error but significantly degrades Clark distance. On Emotion6 the only reliable change is Clark distance, the metric it loses on Flickr-LDL. We do not aggregate the two into a single favourable summary. What replicates on both is the division of labour the decoupling predicts, with SLR-C moving the pointwise-fit metrics and gated UTD moving rank correlation, in a setting where the two cannot share a fitted threshold.

\cresp{The paper lacks theoretical analysis or deeper learning insights. It must add more value to the paper if more theoretical analysis were conducted, e.g., from information-theoretical or feature selection perspectives.}
{\gist{We agree, and rebuilt Sec.~\ref{sec:decoupling_principle} around exactly those two perspectives, with Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} testing what they predict.}

\rh{Information-theoretic side.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy_decomp} treats the intent label as a random human judgement rather than a deterministic ground truth, giving the exact identity $H(Y_c\mid Z)=H(Y_c\mid X)+I(Y_c;X\mid Z)$, which adds irreducible subjective disagreement to the intent-relevant information the representation discards, the latter unrecoverable downstream by the data-processing inequality. SLR-C's contribution is then stated as a conditional-information claim, $H(Y_c\mid B,S)=H(Y_c\mid B)-I(Y_c;S\mid B)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmi_prior}), where the gain is exactly the label-relevant information in the prior that the baseline prediction does not already carry, and vanishes when the prior is redundant. That is why SLR-C supplements under-used semantic information rather than offering a second opinion on the same evidence, and the required non-redundancy is visible in the prior ablation (Table~\ref{tab:prior_ablation}), where no single source dominates.

\rh{Feature-selection side.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin_identity} formalizes the Top-$K$ stage as local hypothesis screening followed by margin correction. Inside the screened set, reranking shifts a confusable pair's margin by exactly $\alpha(\tilde{s}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{c^+}-\tilde{s}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{c^-})$, and nothing outside it changes. In screening terms $K$ trades coverage against distractor noise, and both ends are measured. The baseline Top-10 set covers \res{$83.9\%$} of positive labels, and the sensitivity study (Table~\ref{tab:k_sensitivity_test}) shows an interior optimum at $K=10$.

\rh{UTD as reliability-weighted privileged information.} The rationale teacher is now stated for what it is, a channel available in training and discarded at test time~\cite{vapnik_new_2009,lopez-paz_unifying_2016}. That turns the gate into an estimation question with an answer, because the mean-squared-error-optimal weight $g^{\ast}=\sigma_v^2(\omega)/(\sigma_v^2(\omega)+\sigma_t^2+b_t^2)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opt_gate} is monotonically decreasing in agreement, so our $g=1-\omega$ is a parameter-free monotone approximation of it rather than an unstructured heuristic.

\rh{The experiment these derivations predict.} Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} stratifies samples by the two ambiguities and measures each module per cell, cross-fitted over the train split because it is the only split carrying per-sample agreement. The semantic axis confirms the prediction cleanly. The supervisory axis is directionally consistent but weaker, and its low-ambiguity stratum holds only $1{,}859$ images, so we claim no monotone trend. The full model also does not exceed its stronger component in the doubly-ambiguous cell. We therefore use the experiment to support the predicted localization of the two effects, not to claim a gain from composing them.

\cresp{The overall framework is over-complicated and lacks clarity in design justification. Multiple modules are introduced, but their individual contributions and interactions are not clearly disentangled. The ablation studies, while present, are not sufficient to demonstrate that each component is necessary rather than redundant, making the method appear over-engineered.}
{\gist{FDIL has two functional modules, only one of which exists at inference, and four independent tests separate necessity from redundancy, two of them reproduced below.}

\rh{On complexity.} There are two modules, not many, namely a fixed semantic prior with a residual head (SLR-C) and a training-only text teacher (UTD). At inference UTD contributes nothing, because the teacher is discarded, so the deployed model is a lightweight residual MLP over frozen features at \res{$2.41$}M parameters and \res{$0.47$}\,ms per image (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_comparison}).

\rh{On necessity rather than redundancy.} The manuscript offers four independent tests. The unified control shows that merging both signals into one distillation target performs no better than either alone (Table~\ref{tab:unified_decoupled}). Shuffling rationales across images is worse than removing UTD entirely (\res{$-10.40$} against \res{$-4.47$} Avg.\ F1), so the gain depends on image-specific content rather than on adding external text (Table~\ref{tab:negative_controls}). Removing or flattening the agreement gate degrades results consistently (\res{$-1.97$} and \res{$-1.12$} Avg.\ F1). And the two label-distribution transfers separate the modules by which metric each one moves, on both datasets, where they cannot share a fitted threshold. Redundant components do not act on different quantities.

\rh{Where a component is not clearly necessary.} FDIL's advantage over its own UTD-only ablation does not reach significance on Samples F1 at $n=5$ ($p_{\mathrm H}=0.0755$ in Table~\ref{tab:significance_letter}), although it does on Macro, Micro, Avg., mAP, and hard-subset F1. The case for the decoupled design therefore rests on the overall evidence together with modularity, since each function stays separately measurable and UTD can be deployed alone at zero additional inference cost.

\rh{Presentation.} The unified-control and interaction experiments are now one subsection arguing effectiveness and necessity together, and every results subsection has been reduced to its table or figure, a short analysis, and a conclusion.

\rh{One note on notation.} The notation table added in the previous round has been withdrawn, purely because the page limit under double spacing left no room once the new theoretical section, the interaction experiment, and the cross-dataset study entered the manuscript. No symbol became less explicit, because each is introduced in words at its first occurrence, including those the table used to carry, and we would gladly reinstate it if the editor can grant the additional page.
