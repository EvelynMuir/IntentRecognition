\cresp{There should be larger-scale datasets to better evaluate the proposed methods.}
{We share the reviewer's view that evidence beyond Intentonomy is useful for assessing the scope of the proposed method. To this end, we add an auxiliary cross-dataset study on EMOTIC and report the full results here. Since multi-annotator agreement is available only for the EMOTIC validation and test partitions, we pool these partitions and conduct a controlled $5\times5$ cross-validation study over 10,613 persons, using out-of-fold teacher predictions to avoid target leakage. EMOTIC differs from Intentonomy in that it focuses on affective categories rather than visual-intention categories; therefore, we use it to examine transfer to a related subjective social-understanding task, rather than as a second in-domain benchmark.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:emotic_pool}, FDIL still shows a degree of transferability on EMOTIC. Across 25 seed$\times$fold runs, full FDIL improves the baseline by \res{$+0.18$ Macro-F1}, \res{$+0.50$ Micro-F1}, \res{$+0.40$ Samples-F1}, and \res{$+0.18$ mAP}. The gains on Micro-F1 and mAP are significant at $p<0.01$, the Samples-F1 gain reaches $p=0.0255$, and the Macro-F1 gain is positive but not statistically significant. The ablation results further show that agreement-gated UTD is the main source of transfer: UTD-only obtains the best Macro-, Micro-, Samples-, and Hard-F1, while full FDIL obtains the best mAP and remains statistically comparable to UTD-only on all aggregate metrics. In contrast, SLR-C alone is less effective on EMOTIC, which is expected given the gap between EMOTIC's affective label space and the visual-intention taxonomy targeted by our semantic prior.

The agreement-stratified results in Table~\ref{tab:emotic_agreement} provide a more detailed picture. FDIL yields reliable gains on less-than-fully-agreed samples, improving Micro-F1 by \res{$+0.50$} at agreement 0.33 ($p=0.0045$) and mAP by \res{$+0.58$} at agreement 0.66 ($p=0.0006$). No reliable gain is observed on fully agreed samples, suggesting that the benefit of uncertainty-guided supervision is more evident when annotator disagreement is present. Overall, these results indicate that FDIL has a degree of generalization beyond visual intention recognition, mainly through its uncertainty-guided supervision component, while also revealing that SLR-C depends on the compatibility between textual label semantics and the target taxonomy. Because EMOTIC differs from our main task setting and our controlled split is not comparable with the official EMOTIC train/validation/test protocol or published baselines, we add this cross-dataset evidence and its limitations to the Discussion without presenting it as a new EMOTIC benchmark result.
