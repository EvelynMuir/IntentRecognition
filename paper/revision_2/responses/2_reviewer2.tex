We thank the reviewer for acknowledging the improvements in methodological detail, evaluation, and presentation, and for stating the remaining concerns so precisely. All four are addressed with changes in the manuscript, summarized below.}

\cresp{Nevertheless, there are still some serious scientific concerns. There is still a lack of theoretical justification of functionally separate two types of ambiguity.}
{\gist{Sec.~\ref{sec:decoupling_principle} has been rebuilt around an exact identity plus one derivation per module, and Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} adds an experiment that tests what they predict.}

We agree, and have rebuilt the analysis on an exact footing. Sec.~\ref{sec:decoupling_principle} now derives the exact three-term identity $H(Y_c\mid B)=H(Y_c\mid X)+I(Y_c;X\mid Z)+I(Y_c;Z\mid B)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy_decomp}), which separates irreducible annotator disagreement, label-relevant information the frozen representation discards, and information the baseline readout leaves unused. The middle term is unrecoverable downstream, and since our text prior is itself computed from the same frozen feature it cannot address it; what SLR-C recovers is bounded by the third term. Stating the decomposition this way removes an inconsistency in the previous two-term version, which could be read as claiming that a module reading the same representation recovers what that representation had already discarded. Each module is then characterized on its own terms. SLR-C is stated through the conditional mutual information it supplies (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmi_prior}) and the margin it shifts inside the screened candidate set (Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin_identity}). UTD is stated as learning using privileged information, whose mean-squared-error-optimal weight decreases in annotator agreement and so motivates the gate $1-\omega$ (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:opt_gate}--\eqref{eq:gate_grad}).

It establishes that the terms are functionals of different objects, namely the annotation conditional, the representation map, and the readout, and which motivates treating them with distinct mechanisms; whether that separation helps in practice is an empirical question, and under the unified control we test (Table~\ref{tab:unified_decoupled}) the decoupled design is better. We are careful not to claim that both ambiguities can be reduced: the irreducible term admits no reduction by any learner, and UTD's role is to limit target-estimation error and avoid overfitting unreliable hard targets, not to lower it. Whether that pays off is empirical, so we added an experiment to test it. The new $2\times2$ ambiguity-stratified analysis below confirms the prediction on the semantic axis, where SLR-C gains only in the high-semantic-ambiguity cells, and shows UTD inert wherever annotator agreement is high, with its only significant gain where both ambiguities coincide. The full model does not dominate the doubly-ambiguous cell, so we claim the predicted localization of the two effects rather than a gain from composing them.

\cresp{Moreover, the evaluation is limited to the single benchmark dataset that makes it impossible to assess generalization ability of the approach.}
{\gist{We agree, and the manuscript now carries a cross-dataset study on the larger EMOTIC benchmark and on two label-distribution benchmarks.}

Sec.~\ref{sec:crossdataset} adds two kinds of transfer. Sec.~\ref{sec:emotic} evaluates the larger EMOTIC benchmark under a controlled $5\times5$ cross-validation on its multi-annotated pool, with five seed-level paired observations, where every aggregate metric improves significantly over the matched baseline. Sec.~\ref{sec:flickrldl} then evaluates Flickr-LDL ($11{,}150$ images) and Emotion6 ($1{,}980$ images). In our implementation, each uses its untouched official test split over five seeds with paired $t$-tests. These vary a different axis than a further multi-label dataset would. Label distribution learning replaces independent per-class decisions with a point on the probability simplex, so the threshold search disappears and only the two functional roles remain.

\rh{Results.} On Flickr-LDL, FDIL significantly improves rank correlation and the divisiveness error but significantly degrades Clark distance. On Emotion6 the only reliable change is Clark distance, the very metric it loses on Flickr-LDL. The margins are small and Emotion6's $594$-image test split has little power at this effect size, so we do not present either dataset as a new state of the art for label distribution learning. Four published label-distribution objectives, $\delta$-LDL~\cite{li_approximately_2025}, LDL-LRR~\cite{jia_label_2023}, LDL-DPA~\cite{jia_adaptive_2024}, and LDL-DVS~\cite{lu2026divisiveness}, run at their default weights on identical features, splits, seeds, and head, all produce only negligible changes relative to the matched baseline (Table~\ref{tab:ldl_letter}), which also limits what those comparisons can establish.

\rh{What we do take from the study.} The division of labour replicates on both datasets. SLR-C primarily affects the pointwise-fit metrics (KLD, cosine, $\mu$), while gated UTD moves rank correlation and leaves KLD no better. That separation holds where the two modules cannot share a fitted threshold, which is the most plausible way it could otherwise have been an artefact. Table~\ref{tab:ldl_letter} below gives the seed dispersion, the four published comparators, and the per-metric $p$-values that the manuscript table omits for space.
