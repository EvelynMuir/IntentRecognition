\cresp{There must be larger-scale datasets to better evaluate the proposed methods.}
{\gist{The manuscript now evaluates the larger EMOTIC benchmark, plus two label-distribution benchmarks that broaden the evaluation along a second axis.}

\rh{Scale.} Sec.~\ref{sec:emotic} adds EMOTIC~\cite{kosti_context_2019}, with \res{$23{,}571$} images and \res{$34{,}320$} annotated instances. Full FDIL improves the baseline by \res{$+0.42$} Macro, \res{$+0.67$} Micro, \res{$+0.62$} Samples F1, and \res{$+0.38$} mAP, each significant at $p\le0.005$. The division of labour is the same as on Intentonomy. Gated UTD carries the thresholded F1 metrics, and SLR-C carries mAP. Only SLR-C's candidate size and prior weight are retuned for the new label space (\res{$K=26$}, \res{$\alpha=0.09$}).

\rh{Breadth.} Sec.~\ref{sec:flickrldl} then evaluates Flickr-LDL (\res{$11{,}150$} images, annotator vote distributions over eight emotions) and Emotion6 (\res{$1{,}980$} images, seven categories including neutral). These datasets answer breadth rather than scale. Label distribution learning removes the class-wise threshold search entirely, so only the two functional roles are left to carry the transfer. We run four published label-distribution objectives, $\delta$-LDL~\cite{li_approximately_2025}, LDL-LRR~\cite{jia_label_2023}, LDL-DPA~\cite{jia_adaptive_2024}, and LDL-DVS~\cite{lu2026divisiveness}. All four stay essentially at the baseline under strong frozen CLIP features (Table~\ref{tab:ldl_transfer}). FDIL gains significantly on both. It improves rank correlation and divisiveness error on Flickr-LDL, and Clark distance on Emotion6. What replicates on both is the division of labour, with SLR-C moving the pointwise-fit metrics and gated UTD moving rank correlation. Table~\ref{tab:ldl_letter} of this letter adds the seed dispersion and per-metric $p$-values.

\cresp{The paper lacks theoretical analysis or deeper learning insights. It must add more value to the paper if more theoretical analysis were conducted, e.g., from information-theoretical or feature selection perspectives.}
{\gist{Sec.~\ref{sec:decoupling_principle} is rebuilt around exactly those two perspectives, and Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} tests what they predict.}

\rh{Information-theoretic side.} We treat the intent label as a random human judgement rather than a deterministic ground truth. That gives the exact identity $H(Y_c\mid B)=H(Y_c\mid X)+I(Y_c;X\mid Z)+I(Y_c;Z\mid B)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy_decomp}). It separates irreducible subjective disagreement, the intent-relevant information the frozen representation discards, and the information the baseline readout leaves unused. By the data-processing inequality the middle term is unrecoverable downstream. SLR-C's contribution is then a conditional-information claim, $H(Y_c\mid B,S)=H(Y_c\mid B)-I(Y_c;S\mid B)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmi_prior}). The prior is computed from the same frozen feature, so this gain is bounded by $I(Y_c;S\mid B)\le I(Y_c;Z\mid B)$. The gain vanishes when the prior is redundant given the baseline scores. SLR-C's measured improvement therefore says the prior supplies additional label-relevant information, and the prior ablation (Table~\ref{tab:prior_ablation}), where no single source dominates, says the three textual sources contribute complementary content.

\rh{Feature-selection side.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin_identity} formalizes the top-$K$ stage as candidate screening followed by margin correction. Inside the screened set, reranking shifts a confusable pair's margin by exactly $\alpha(\tilde{s}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{c^+}-\tilde{s}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{c^-})$, and nothing outside it changes. The remaining intents are therefore protected by construction, not by an accuracy assumption on the prior. In screening terms $K$ trades coverage against distractor noise, and we measure both ends. The baseline top-10 set covers \res{$83.9\%$} of positive labels, and the sensitivity study (Table~\ref{tab:k_sensitivity_test}) shows an interior optimum at $K=10$.

\rh{UTD as reliability-weighted privileged information.} The rationale teacher is now stated for what it is, a channel available in training and discarded at test time~\cite{vapnik_new_2009,lopez-paz_unifying_2016}. That turns the gate into an estimation question with an answer. The MSE-optimal weight $g^{\ast}=\sigma_v^2(\omega)/(\sigma_v^2(\omega)+\sigma_t^2+b_t^2)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opt_gate} decreases monotonically in agreement. Our $g=1-\omega$ is therefore a parameter-free monotone surrogate for it, rather than an unstructured heuristic. We adopt the surrogate because $\sigma_v^2(\omega)$, $\sigma_t^2$, and $b_t$ are not observable per sample.

\rh{The experiment these derivations predict.} Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} stratifies samples by the two ambiguities and measures each module per cell. The semantic axis confirms the prediction cleanly. The supervisory axis is consistent with it, with the caveat that its low-ambiguity stratum holds only $1{,}859$ images. In the doubly-ambiguous cell the full model matches its stronger component rather than exceeding it. The experiment therefore supports the predicted localization of the two effects, not a gain from composing them.

\cresp{The overall framework is over-complicated and lacks clarity in design justification. Multiple modules are introduced, but their individual contributions and interactions are not clearly disentangled. The ablation studies, while present, are not sufficient to demonstrate that each component is necessary rather than redundant, making the method appear over-engineered.}
{\gist{FDIL has two functional modules, only one of which exists at inference, and each is dictated by a distinct term of the decomposition. The $2\times2$ analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} disentangles their individual contributions, and four further tests separate necessity from redundancy.}

\rh{Complexity and design justification.} FDIL has two modules, a fixed semantic prior with a residual head (SLR-C) and a training-only text teacher (UTD). The teacher is discarded after training. The deployed model is therefore a lightweight residual MLP over frozen features, at \res{$2.41$}M parameters and \res{$0.47$}\,ms per image (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_comparison}). The two modules are not accumulated components. They are the two quantities Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy_decomp} leaves actionable. Readout insufficiency $I(Y_c;Z\mid B)$ is a property of the head given a frozen representation. SLR-C acts on it, by supplying conditional information from a fixed prior (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmi_prior}) and correcting margins inside the screened set (Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin_identity}). Unreliable annotation is instead a property of the supervision. It is handled by treating rationales as privileged information, whose optimal weight decreases in agreement (Eq.~\eqref{eq:opt_gate}). One module acts at inference on scores, the other during training on targets. That is why they are given distinct mechanisms rather than one shared target, and the unified controls of Table~\ref{tab:unified_decoupled} confirm the choice empirically.

\rh{Disentangling individual contributions.} The $2\times2$ analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrants} is added for this purpose. It stratifies samples by semantic ambiguity, using the negative top-2 margin of out-of-fold baseline logits, and by supervisory ambiguity, using $1-\omega$. Each module is then measured per cell against a shared baseline, with paired $t$-tests and Holm correction over the twelve comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:quadrants}, reproduced in the Reviewer~2 block above). The modules localize to different cells. SLR-C gains only where semantic ambiguity is high (\res{$+4.08$} and \res{$+1.17$} F1, both significant) and nothing in the low-semantic cells, so its effect tracks the margin axis independently of agreement. UTD is inert wherever agreement is high (\res{$-0.05$} and \res{$-0.07$} F1), and its only significant gain is the doubly-ambiguous cell (\res{$+0.84$}, corrected $p=0.025$). In that cell the full model matches SLR-C alone (\res{$+1.14$} against \res{$+1.17$}). We therefore claim the predicted localization, not additive composition.

\rh{Necessity rather than redundancy.} Four further tests bear on this. First, merging both signals into one distillation target performs no better than either alone (Table~\ref{tab:unified_decoupled}). Second, shuffling rationales across images is worse than removing UTD entirely (\res{$-10.40$} against \res{$-4.47$} Avg.\ F1), so the gain depends on image-specific content rather than on adding external text (Table~\ref{tab:negative_controls}). Third, removing or flattening the agreement gate costs \res{$-1.97$} and \res{$-1.12$} Avg.\ F1 and degrades every other aggregate metric; the uniform gate gains \res{$+1.13$} on the hard subset alone. Fourth, the two label-distribution transfers separate the modules by which metric each one moves, on both datasets, where they cannot share a fitted threshold. FDIL's advantage over its own UTD-only ablation reaches significance on Macro, Micro, Avg., mAP, and hard-subset F1, but not on Samples F1 at $n=5$ ($p_{\mathrm H}=0.0755$, Table~\ref{tab:significance_letter}). The decoupled design also buys modularity. Each function stays separately measurable, and UTD can be deployed alone at zero additional inference cost.
