\reviewerblock{Reviewer~6}{%
We thank the reviewer for the positive assessment of the writing and structure, and for the precise methodological requirement, which we implement in full.}

\cresp{The paper presents a functionally decoupled framework for visual-intent recognition that addresses supervisory and semantic ambiguity using structured semantic priors and uncertainty-aware distillation. The manuscript is well written, clearly structured, and the proposed contribution is reasonably good and relevant to the field. However, the performance improvement over comparable state-of-the-art methods is relatively small, which limits the strength of the empirical contribution. Although the authors report results using mean $\pm$ standard deviation over three runs, this alone is not sufficiently convincing for establishing statistical superiority. An explicit paired t-test, applied consistently against the strongest state-of-the-art methods, should therefore be provided to demonstrate that the observed improvements are statistically significant rather than caused by random variation.}
{\gist{We implement the requested test, using five seeds with Holm-corrected paired two-sided $t$-tests against every matched-protocol comparator (Table~\ref{tab:significance_letter} of this letter). We also strengthen the empirical contribution itself, with a cross-dataset study and an extended comparison set.}

\rh{Protocol.} We extend from three to \res{five} independent seeds. On each of those seeds we also retrain the matched-protocol reproductions of the three strongest comparable methods, HLEG, LabCR, and IntCLIP. All use identical frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 features, an identical split, and identical validation-only class-wise thresholding. This gives five \emph{paired} observations per method, with the training run as the pairing unit, so a paired two-sided $t$-test on the per-seed differences is well defined. FDIL is tested against five methods per metric, so we also report Holm-corrected $p$-values.

\rh{Outcome.} Avg.\ F1, mAP, and hard-subset F1 are significant against every matched-protocol comparator after correction. The published-only rows are quoted from their papers, and hence lie outside the paired design. Against the strongest compared method, LabCR, the gains are \res{$+3.32$} Avg.\ F1 and \res{$+7.67$} hard-subset F1. The class-wise-thresholded F1 metrics carry more seed-to-seed variance, because thresholds are fitted on a $498$-image validation split. One contrast is not significant, namely Samples F1 against our own UTD-only ablation ($p_{\mathrm H}=0.0755$). The gains over the matched published methods are therefore statistically reliable.

\rh{Beyond the requested test.} The concern is ultimately about the strength of the empirical contribution, so we also broaden the evidence itself. Sec.~\ref{sec:crossdataset} adds a controlled $5\times5$ cross-validation on the larger EMOTIC benchmark, where every aggregate metric improves significantly. It also adds a transfer to Flickr-LDL and Emotion6, where the threshold search disappears entirely and the division of labour between the two modules still replicates. The comparison set gains two 2026 intent-recognition methods, CoT4Intent and HVU-CLIP (TPAMI 2026), the latter also under a same-backbone ResNet101 comparison. Four recent multimodal large models are added in a dedicated zero-shot table.
